# Local variational field theory: finite jets, Euler operators, and Noether identities

This roadmap owns the finite-order local variational calculus for smooth fields: jet-local densities, their Euler expressions and boundary terms, differential identities, symmetry currents, gauge identities, and metric variation. It treats actions only on specified domains with stated support/boundary hypotheses. It does not assert path-integral measures, existence of global solutions, or minimizers. Physical equations are model assumptions or consequences of an explicitly chosen Lagrangian; they are not universal mathematical axioms.

## Mathematical carriers and conventions

Work on a smooth fiber bundle `π:E→M` with finite-dimensional smooth fiber F;
fields are local smooth sections. Consume smooth bundle charts, sections, vertical
tangent bundles, forms and densities from [DifferentialGeometry](../DifferentialGeometry/README.md)
and [SmoothBundlesConnections](../SmoothBundlesConnections/README.md).
LVF1 constructs the jets of this general fiber bundle. Compare its vector-bundle
specialization with the linear jet bundles and differential operators owned by
[PseudodifferentialOperators P1.0](../PseudodifferentialOperators/README.md#p1-differential-operators-jets-and-symbols).
P1.0's formal transpose covers smooth base coefficients; this roadmap owns the
jet-dependent formal-adjoint calculus and its comparison after evaluation on a section.

For an N-dimensional fiber over an n-manifold, use symmetric multi-indices (equivalently, unordered multisets of base indices) for jet coordinates. A derivative indexed by an ordered tuple is the same coordinate, not a distinct jet variable; translate the ordered-index sums in Borcherds p.37 by the corresponding multiplicities. Write `u^a_I`, `|I|≤r`, for coordinates of `J^rE`, and `D_μ u^a_I=u^a_(I+e_μ)`. Let `θ^a_I=du^a_I−Σ_μ u^a_(I+e_μ)dx^μ`. The contact generator with `|I|=r` is realized on `J^(r+1)E`; its exterior derivative and contact splitting are realized on `J^(r+2)E`.

Define the finite-stage modules `Ω_r^{p,q}` by local coefficients on `J^rE`, horizontal generators `dx^μ`, and contact generators `θ_I` of order at most r, with the above raised-stage realization. Transition maps are pullback along truncations. At finite stages,

`d_H f = Σ_μ dx^μ D_μ f`, `d_H dx^μ=0`, `d_H θ_I=Σ_μ dx^μ∧θ_(I+e_μ)`;

`d_V f=Σ_(a,I)(∂f/∂u^a_I)θ^a_I`, `d_V dx^μ=0`, `d_V θ_I=0`.

The sign is fixed by these equations and graded Leibniz. Stagewise `d_H` raises coefficient/contact order at most one, `d_V` stays at the coefficient stage while its ordinary realization uses the raised jet stage, and the pullback squares commute. Thus the filtered colimit of stages has `d_H²=d_V²=d_Hd_V+d_Vd_H=0`; no fixed finite stage is claimed closed. For `n≥1` and at least one field component, the finite-order presheaf `U↦colim_r Ω_r(U)` need not be a sheaf on noncompact U. Use open subsets of the total field bundle E as the base of the finite-order presheaf: stage-r forms lie over their inverse images under `J^rE→E`. The global carrier is its sheafification on E, whose sections are locally finite-order forms. Prove bounded order on each compact subset of E and a finite-stage representative on a neighborhood of that compact; relatively compact opens have this bound when the section is defined on a neighborhood of their closure. No uniform bound is claimed on an arbitrary base-open subset containing unbounded field values. State whether each theorem concerns a finite stage, the locally finite-order sheaf, or compact-supported sections.

On an oriented chart the Lagrangian is `L dⁿx`, a top form. On an unoriented base it is a density. The Euler target is a section over `J^{2r}E` of the pullback of the dual vertical-field bundle tensored with the density line, globally; on an oriented base identify that line with `ΛⁿT*M` by the selected orientation and positive density. Currents and horizontal forms in degree n−1 use `Λ^(n−1)T*M ⊗ o_M` (equivalently the density-twisted horizontal complex), not untwisted forms. Prove the density comparison, twisted Stokes theorem, and the orientation-chart sign. The density-twisted global obstruction is `Hⁿ(E;π^*o_M)`. For a vector bundle, prove that its zero section and fiberwise contraction induce the comparison with `Hⁿ(M;o_M)`; for other contractible fibers, construct the requisite homotopy-equivalence comparison before using the base obstruction. Do not replace the twisted cohomology by ordinary de Rham cohomology on nonorientable M. For the separate untwisted top-form complex, state the corresponding `Hⁿ_dR(E)` obstruction only on its own carrier.

For even real fields the Euler expression is `E_a(L)=Σ_I (−D)_I(∂L/∂u^a_I)`. For a complex field use its underlying real field pair; where the source varies `ψ, ψ̄` independently, prove the equivalent Wirtinger equations for a real density modulo a horizontal divergence. In particular, state and prove `Re L = L + d_H B` for the non-Hermitian Dirac density in the chosen convention. For odd fields fix the coefficient algebra `C∞(even jets)⊗Λ(odd jet coordinates)` at each finite order, left/right derivatives, and the bigraded Koszul sign (including the `(-1)^(pq+ab)` convention when bidegrees are used). Odd first variation is an algebraic identity with Grassmann-valued variation parameters; do not apply the real-valued fundamental lemma or speak of characteristic-zero coefficients of a real algebra. Prove chart independence, `E∘d_H=0`, and the Euler chain rule through the algebraic interior Euler projection over this coefficient algebra (`I²=I`, `I∘d_H=0`, `E=I∘d_V`), not by the real fundamental lemma. Pin the graded involution and reversal rule, including `(θ₁θ₂)^*=θ₂^*θ₁^*`, before taking real parts of odd Dirac densities. Distinguish commuting spinors from Grassmann-valued fields.

## LVF1. Jet-local differential algebra

Construct `J^rE` from equivalence classes of local smooth sections at x: two
sections have the same value and the same symmetric derivatives through order r
in a bundle chart. Prove independence of base and fiber charts using the full
chain rule, including nonlinear fiber-coordinate changes depending on x.
Build the smooth finite-dimensional bundle charts from these derivative tuples,
their Faà di Bruno transitions and cocycles. Prove `J^0E=E`, smooth surjective
truncations, their composition laws and compatibility with restriction and
smooth bundle isomorphisms. For r≥1, prove `J^rE→J^(r−1)E` is an affine bundle
modeled on the pullback of `Sym^r(T*M)⊗VE`; the vertical tangent is taken at the
underlying field value in E. The top-order difference of two jets is this tensor,
while lower-order nonlinear terms are fixed. No vector-space structure or
canonical zero jet is imposed on a general fiber bundle. A connection gives a
first-jet splitting; prove its transformation and difference laws and distinguish
it from a canonical splitting. For a vector bundle prove the comparison with
P1.0's linear jet construction, its truncation sequences and differential-operator
factorization. Define holonomic prolongation `j^r s`, prove its local chain rule
and restriction identities, and characterize the contact ideal by its pullback
vanishing on every prolonged local section, with the required raised jet stage.

Build the finite-stage field algebra, horizontal/contact forms, symmetric-index operations, pullback maps, and a locally finite-order sheaf via sheafification of the filtered presheaf. Prove bounded order on compact subsets of E with the specified neighborhood hypothesis, restriction, coordinate-change, and product compatibility. Define the prolongation of sections and prove jet-surjectivity: for each `z∈J^k_xE` a local smooth section has jet z (using a trivialization and a polynomial with a bump function). Define the stagewise differentials above and prove transition compatibility, bicomplex identities, and finite stage-order estimates. Include the polynomial model using either `MvPolynomial` over the full indexed family of field-jet variables or explicit stage algebras with transition maps: a fixed finite variable set is not closed under total derivatives. Define total derivatives as derivations raising the stage, then construct the polynomial Kähler differentials and the exterior/de Rham complex as an LVF-owned layer (Mathlib’s Kähler differential carrier alone is not a de Rham complex). Map polynomial expressions into the smooth jet algebra and state precisely which identities are preserved. This is a model, not an equality of polynomial and smooth theories.

## LVF2. First variation and Euler operator

For order-r `L`, define the first variation density and the section `E(L)∈V(E)^*⊗Density(M)` using repeated finite multi-index integration by parts. Prove the order bound `ord E(L)≤2r`. Prove the coordinate identity

`δL = ⟨E(L),δu⟩ + d_H Θ_L`,

where each term is a density-valued horizontal n-form; keep the integral/action equation separate and add compact support or boundary integrals only when used. Boundary terms are local choices; compute their overlap and improvement law. Define a Lepage equivalent ρ as an n-form on a specified finite jet stage (or locally finite-order carrier) whose horizontalization is λ and whose one-contact component of dρ is the Euler source form. Construct it globally and prove gluing, the contact-component identities and explicit order bounds. Give the canonical first-order Poincaré–Cartan form on `J¹E` and its contact structure. Distinguish this full Lepage class from the boundary form `Θ∈Ω^{n−1,1}` in the first-variation identity. In the locally finite-order boundary-form class, local positive-contact exactness makes Θ unique for n=1 and gives the ambiguity `Θ↦Θ+d_Hη`, `η∈Ω^{n−2,1}`, for n≥2, already for first-order L when no order restriction is imposed. In base dimension n=1 an n-form has no two-contact component: in this full Lepage class, the difference is a `(0,1)`-form with zero d_H, so positive-contact exactness at horizontal degree zero gives uniqueness. For n≥2, adding an n-form of contact degree at least two also preserves the defining horizontal and one-contact components. State uniqueness of any selected finite-order/contact-normalized representative within its explicitly defined class, and derive it from the coefficient identities.

For even commuting fields, prove the fundamental lemma for continuous coefficients against all compactly supported smooth tests, componentwise in local frames. Prove uniqueness of source forms from that lemma and jet-surjectivity; use uniqueness for chart independence, `E(d_HB)=0`, and the Euler chain rule. For odd fields use the algebraic interior-Euler argument specified above instead. For the local converse, state the horizontal homotopy with both bidegrees. On a local bundle trivialization restricted to a star-shaped base ball and a star-shaped fiber-coordinate patch, and for **positive contact degree** `q≥1`, the horizontal rows are locally exact below top degree: for `0≤p<n`, `h_H^{p+1,q}d_H+d_Hh_H^{p,q}=id`. At `p=0` this formula is valid because `q≥1`; do not extend it to the unaugmented zero-contact row, where `d_H`-closed horizontal functions include constants and the row begins with `ℝ→Ω^{0,0}`. At top horizontal degree, use the augmented contact row `Ω^{n−1,q}→Ω^{n,q}→F_q→0`: `ω=I_q(ω)+d_H h_H^{n,q}ω`, with `I_q²=I_q`, `I_q d_H=0`, and `F_q=im I_q`. Thus `ker I_q=im d_H` at top degree; do not say the unaugmented top contact row is acyclic. For zero contact degree, the top row is augmented by the Euler operator `E:Ω^{n,0}→F₁`, with `E∘d_H=0`; on these star-shaped total-space patches and for `n>0`, `ker E=im d_H`. Its bottom augmentation is the locally constant functions. In contact degree one, the interior Euler projection `I₁:Ω^{n,1}→F₁` has image the source forms and `E=I₁∘d_V`. On such a fiber patch, choose its center and the vertical-fiber scaling cylinder `s:[0,1]×J→J`; global scaling of a general fiber bundle is not assumed. Define `Kω=∫₀¹ι_(∂t)s*ωdt` and prove `d_VK+Kd_V=id−s₀*` and `Kd_H=−d_HK`. Combine this with the base radial homotopy on a star-shaped coordinate ball to prove `E(L)=0 ⇒ L=L₀(x)+d_HB`; for n>0, `L₀` is locally exact, while n=0 is separately stated.

Globalize on the locally finite-order sheaf on E using these total-space patches. For `q≥1`, prove exactness of the contact row through its image `F_q=im I_q`, including `ker I_q=im d_H` at top degree. For `q=0`, the Euler–Lagrange complex is `ℝ→Ω^{0,0}→⋯→Ω^{n,0}→F₁→F₂→⋯`, where the next map `F₁→F₂` is the Helmholtz operator. Prove exactness through `Ω^{n,0}` (`ker E=im d_H` locally); do not assert `E:Ω^{n,0}→F₁` is onto, since Helmholtz conditions make its image proper. Prove the relevant sheaves fine and the asserted rows exact before using fine-sheaf acyclicity; fineness alone does not make an unaugmented complex exact. Use a global Lepage construction and [SmoothBundlesConnections B6.0–B6.1](../SmoothBundlesConnections/README.md#b6-circle-bundles-integral-classes-and-flat-ambiguity)'s locally finite good covers and Čech–de Rham double complex `D=δ+(-1)^p d`. Keep two global complexes separate. For the untwisted complex on a fiber bundle `π:E→M`, horizontal cohomology below top degree is `H^p_dR(E)` and the Euler–Lagrange complex has top variational cohomology `ker(E:Ω^{n,0}→F₁)/im(d_H:Ω^{n−1,0}→Ω^{n,0})≅H^n_{dR}(E)`; the raw quotient `Ω^{n,0}/im d_H` is not this cohomology. For density-valued Lagrangians and currents on an unoriented base, use the orientation-local-system complex twisted by `π^*o_M`; its horizontal cohomology below top degree is `H^p(E;π^*o_M)` and its top variational cohomology is `H^n(E;π^*o_M)`, which for a vector bundle is `H^n(M;o_M)`. On an oriented base the selected orientation trivializes the twist and recovers the untwisted statement. The density-twisted Čech–de Rham comparison is an LVF-owned local-system construction; B6.1 only supplies the constant-coefficient good-cover complex. In both carriers the top obstruction need not vanish. For positive-contact augmented rows, prove exactness at every term including `ker I_q` in top degree, then use the specified locally finite sheaf hypotheses to globalize. Give the mapping-cone/open-pair formulation when relative boundary data are used; do not identify relative forms with a kernel for arbitrary open pairs.

## LVF5. Noether II, reducibility, and identities

Define gauge generators as finite-order differential operators `R` from parameter sections to vertical variations, with the jet-coefficient formal adjoint from LVF3. For compactly supported parameters prove `R*(E(L))=0` off shell iff the variation is a divergence for all parameters, using the fundamental lemma. Write the density identity separately from its integral. In the converse, a syzygy operator `Q` must be differential in its arguments with local coefficients, and its adjoint acts on the arbitrary parameter; spell out supports.

Use three explicit model complexes; their hypotheses are part of the theorem statements, not a generic regularity escape clause.

* **Free Abelian electrodynamics on flat Minkowski space.** For a potential `A∈Ω¹(M)`, take `R(ε)=dε`, `E(A)=δdA`, and the Noether operator `N(E)=δE`; fix the Lorentz metric and formal-adjoint convention so `N∘E=δ²d=0`. The gauge-parameter complex is `ℝ→C∞(M) --d→ Ω¹(M)`, exact at `C∞` on each connected chart; the configuration stabilizer is `ker d`, locally constant parameters; it vanishes under compact support on connected noncompact Minkowski space. This fixed-configuration kernel is not a local reducibility operator. For the constant-coefficient identity calculation, let `S=ℝ[ξ₀,…,ξ₃]`, `q(ξ)=ξ₀²−ξ₁²−ξ₂²−ξ₃²`, and the mixed-index symbol `M(ξ)^μ_ν=q(ξ)δ^μ_ν−ξ^μξ_ν`. Its left kernel is generated by the covector `ξ_μ`. Prove `ker_S(·M)=S·ξ` by cancelling the relatively-prime factors `q` and `ξ_μ`, and prove exactness of the Koszul complex on `(ξ₀,…,ξ₃)` at `S⊗Λ^kℝ⁴` for `k>0`. This fixes the generator and relations of the translation-invariant Noether-identity module; the proof does not claim the same classification for arbitrary variable-coefficient operators without a separate filtered argument.
* **Scalar electrodynamics on a connected Minkowski domain.** Fix `D_μ=∂_μ+ieA_μ` and `δ_εA=dε`, `δ_εφ=−ieεφ`, `δ_εφ̄=ieεφ̄` (or state and prove the equivalent all-sign-reversed convention). For the real action use the paired complex-field Euler expressions and derive the exact identity `−∂_μE_A^μ−ieφE_φ+ieφ̄E_{φ̄}=0` by formal adjunction of this `R_φ`. At a fixed configuration the stabilizer is `{ε : dε=0 ∧ εφ=0}` (with `e≠0`), hence on a connected domain it is the constants `c` with `cφ=0`. Keep this configuration stabilizer separate from local reducibility of the gauge generator.
* **Yang–Mills on a fixed principal bundle.** With connection `A`, curvature `F_A`, invariant pairing, `R_A(ε)=D_Aε`, and `E_A=D_A^*F_A`, derive `D_A^*E_A=0` from gauge invariance/Bianchi with the fixed adjoint convention. At a fixed connection the stabilizer is the parallel-section space `ker D_A`; it vanishes for compact support on a connected noncompact chart, while globally it depends on the stabilizer of A. This configuration stabilizer is distinct from a local differential reducibility operator. Gauge fixing changes both the generator complex and identity module; derive its new complex separately. For boundary parameters, replace compact support by the exact boundary domain and retain the boundary charge term.

For each model define the finite-order local differential-function algebra `F_loc`, the prolonged Euler differential ideal `I_∞=(D_I E_a)`, and its Koszul–Tate complex. A stage-s reducibility operator is a finite-order local differential operator `Z_s` with `R_s∘Z_s=0` identically on the jet algebra (or, in a separately labelled on-shell theory, modulo `I_∞`); its antifield is added only if such an operator is exhibited. Prove that Maxwell, scalar electrodynamics and Yang–Mills have no nonzero local stage-1 reducibility operator. Separately prove vanishing of Koszul–Tate `H₂` as part of the model-specific exactness theorem below: absence of reducibility of the displayed generator does not by itself establish completeness of its identity module or acyclicity of the resolution. For scalar electrodynamics the first component of `R_φ∘Z=0` gives `d∘Z=0` for arbitrary parameter jets, forcing `Z=0`; for Yang–Mills, `D_A∘Z=0` as an identity for arbitrary connection jets forces `Z=0` by its top-order symbol and arbitrary `A`-dependence. Maxwell is the same local-operator argument for `d∘Z=0`. These local facts do not imply zero fixed-configuration stabilizers. The identity `δ_KT²=0` is exactly the displayed Noether identity. The equality `H₀=F_loc/I_∞` is by construction, not an extra hypothesis. The nontrivial KT-regularity obligations are the vanishing of positive-degree homology, with `F_loc`-module generators given by all prolongations `D_I` of the displayed gauge identities; LVF5 owns the exactness proofs under the model’s field, bundle and support hypotheses. Fix the identity carrier before stating completeness: a strict differential identity is an equality of local differential functions `Q(E)=0`, not equality modulo a total divergence. Under the fixed pairing, integration by parts identifies differential-operator identities with cycles in the prolonged Koszul–Tate module generated by all jets `D_Iu_a^*`, whose differential sends `D_Iu_a^*` to `D_IE_a`. Define the trivial syzygy submodule as the image of the degree-two Koszul generators formed only from pairs of equation antifields and their prolongations; these give the strict skew relations among prolonged Euler expressions. Total-divergence terms remain in the separate first-variation/action carrier and are not inserted into this strict syzygy module. LVF5 must prove the operator-to-cycle correspondence, the inclusion of these Koszul boundaries among strict cycles, and compatibility with the chosen support domains. Completeness is the theorem that the quotient of strict cycles by these boundaries is generated by the classes represented by the displayed gauge-identity antifield boundaries `δ_KT(c_A^*)=R_A^*(u^*)`, with all relations supplied by the specified reducibility maps.
